## Private Domestic Leadership Development

**Status:** Working draft

## Purpose

This document details the domain-specific progression ladders for the female partner.  

Each domain moves through the shared five-phase skeleton (Recognition → Experiential Proof → Role Preference → Consolidation → Transfer) at its own pace and with its own methods. A participant will almost never occupy the same phase across all domains simultaneously.

The goal is not uniform advancement. The goal is measurable movement toward the female end-states defined in the parent framework.

---

## Domain 1: Authority & Decision Comfort

**End-state:** Comfortable deciding, directing, and setting expectations for both partners without excessive hesitation or permission-seeking.

### Phase Ladder

**1. Recognition**  
- Identifies patterns of unnecessary consultation or softening of decisions.  
- Begins to notice the cost of constant accommodation.  
- Method focus: language audits, decision diaries, guided reflection.

**2. Experiential Proof**  
- Practices low-stakes unilateral decisions that are immediately complied with.  
- Experiences others adapting to her stated preference in real time.  
- Method focus: structured decision exercises, specialist and staff compliance drills, husband response protocols.

**3. Role Preference**  
- Begins to prefer the clarity and efficiency of deciding rather than negotiating every point.  
- Shows reduced anxiety when her decision affects her partner.  
- Method focus: repeated higher-stakes decisions, positive reinforcement of clear direction, mild public micro-displays of authority.

**4. Consolidation**  
- Uses authoritative language as default (“I have decided…”, “This is what will happen…”).  
- Experiences authority as congruent with her identity rather than a temporary experiment.  
- Method focus: identity language work, reduced external scaffolding, cohort modelling.

**5. Transfer**  
- Articulates specific domains of household, social, and career life in which she will hold final decision rights at home.  
- Method focus: concrete commitment statements, relapse-prevention planning.

**Key early indicators of movement**  
Reduced softening language (“maybe we could…”, “what do you think?”) → clearer directives.

## Domain 2: Self-Prioritisation (Career, Time, Ambition)

**End-state:** Willing to treat her career, time, pleasure, and ambitions as primary resources.

### Phase Ladder

**1. Recognition**  
- Maps how her time and energy have been systematically secondary.  
- Identifies internalised beliefs that her ambitions should yield.  
- Method focus: time-and-attention audits, career-protection reflection.

**2. Experiential Proof**  
- Experiences scheduled blocks in which her work, rest, or pleasure takes absolute priority and others adapt.  
- Observes that the household continues to function when she does not carry the mental load.  
- Method focus: protected time protocols, staff and husband adaptation exercises.

**3. Role Preference**  
- Begins to feel entitled to uninterrupted focus and recovery.  
- Shows irritation or boundary-setting when her priority time is encroached upon.  
- Method focus: repeated priority scheduling, reinforcement of boundary enforcement.

**4. Consolidation**  
- Speaks of her career and time as resources that others are expected to protect.  
- Experiences self-prioritisation as normal rather than selfish.  
- Method focus: identity framing, peer modelling by more advanced women.

**5. Transfer**  
- Defines concrete rules for home (e.g., protected work windows, travel priority, decision rights over her schedule).  
- Method focus: written priority protocols to take home.

## Domain 3: Receiving Support & Reduced Guilt

**End-state:** Able to delegate, be served, and allow her partner to carry responsibility without guilt.

### Phase Ladder

**1. Recognition**  
- Identifies guilt responses when needs are met by others.  
- Names the belief that she should remain the ultimate responsible party.  
- Method focus: guilt-mapping exercises, cognitive labelling of conditioning.

**2. Experiential Proof**  
- Receives graduated service (practical and personal) while being coached to remain receptive rather than reciprocal.  
- Experiences the absence of catastrophe when she does not manage everything.  
- Method focus: structured service protocols from husband, specialist facilitation, immediate guilt interruption.

**3. Role Preference**  
- Begins to prefer being cared for in specific domains.  
- Shows reduced urge to “help” or take back control during service.  
- Method focus: extended service periods, reinforcement of receptive posture.

**4. Consolidation**  
- Experiences being supported as appropriate to her role rather than as a temporary indulgence.  
- Guilt responses become weaker and shorter.  
- Method focus: identity work (“I am someone who is supported”), reduced therapist intervention.

**5. Transfer**  
- Specifies which responsibilities she will permanently release and what standard of support she expects.  
- Method focus: delegation contracts, home implementation planning.

## Domain 4: Sexual Autonomy

**End-state:** Comfortable with her own desire, being desired, and making sexual choices for herself — including sex outside the marriage.

### Phase Ladder

**1. Recognition**  
- Acknowledges the gap between her actual desire patterns and the constrained role she has performed.  
- Begins to separate sexual loyalty from sexual ownership.  
- Method focus: desire mapping, fantasy inventory, shame labelling (non-shaming).

**2. Experiential Proof**  
- Experiences structured sexual encounters with Real Man Specialists under controlled conditions.  
- Practices directing intensity, pace, and acts.  
- Experiences being the sexual priority while her husband is present or in a service role.  
- Method focus: staged specialist sessions, observation of advanced participants, immediate post-session reframing.

**3. Role Preference**  
- Begins to prefer sexual experiences that centre her desire and status.  
- Shows reduced residual guilt and increased anticipatory ownership.  
- Method focus: woman-initiated scheduling of specialists, hierarchical framing during encounters, cohort sexual modelling.

**4. Consolidation**  
- Experiences external sexual choice as a legitimate expression of her autonomy rather than a transgression.  
- Language shifts toward entitlement and selectivity.  
- Method focus: identity integration, reduced novelty framing, emphasis on continuity of choice.

**5. Transfer**  
- Articulates the sexual terms she intends to maintain or expand at home (access rules, external partners, husband’s role).  
- Method focus: explicit sexual protocol design for home use.

**Special notes**  
This domain typically requires the heaviest specialist involvement and the most careful management of husband presence. Group observation and peer modelling are particularly potent here.

## Domain 5: Norm Resistance / Reduced Conditioning

**End-state:** Less ruled by traditional femininity, shame, expected compromise, or fear of judgement.

### Phase Ladder

**1. Recognition**  
- Identifies specific conditioning messages still operating (appearance rules, emotional labour expectations, sexual modesty, conflict avoidance).  
- Method focus: conditioning inventory, language audits.

**2. Experiential Proof**  
- Practices behaviours that violate old norms inside a judgement-free, high-status environment.  
- Observes that feared social consequences do not materialise within the programme.  
- Method focus: controlled norm-violation exercises, immediate social reinforcement, peer modelling.

**3. Role Preference**  
- Begins to prefer the freedom of the new behaviours over the safety of the old ones.  
- Method focus: repeated practice in semi-public programme settings, status reinforcement for non-conforming behaviour.

**4. Consolidation**  
- Experiences the new norms as more accurate to her actual values and power.  
- Residual fear of external judgement decreases.  
- Method focus: identity language, future-pacing against home and professional environments.

**5. Transfer**  
- Identifies which old norms she will actively refuse to re-absorb and what support structures she needs.  
- Method focus: personal norm-refusal list, relapse-prevention planning.

## Cross-Domain Coordination Rules

1. Sexual Autonomy and Authority often accelerate each other once both reach Experiential Proof.  
2. Receiving Support frequently lags behind Authority; guilt must be treated as a separate target.  
3. Self-Prioritisation benefits from early protected-time successes before higher-stakes career framing.  
4. Norm Resistance is continuous background work that supports every other domain.  
5. No domain is advanced by insight alone. Every phase after Recognition requires structured experience.

## Measurement

Each domain is scored on the relevant KPIs from the parent framework at intake, mid-programme, and pre-departure.  
Narrative progress notes focus on language shifts, behavioural initiation, and residual friction points.

Detailed session structures, specialist protocols, and measurement instruments are maintained in the Methods Catalogue.
