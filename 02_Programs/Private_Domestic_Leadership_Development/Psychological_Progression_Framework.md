## Purpose

This document defines the high-level psychological progression system used in Minerva’s Private Domestic Leadership Development programmes (particularly island-based intensives).

It translates Minerva’s core pillars into measurable end-states for both partners and provides a flexible structure for designing interventions.

The system is deliberately **multi-domain**. Different capacities (authority, sexual autonomy, receiving support, etc.) do not progress at the same speed or through identical methods. The shared phase language exists only to allow coordination across domains and between partners.

---

## Ultimate End-States

### Female Partner
The woman becomes:

- **More authoritative** — comfortable deciding, directing, and setting expectations for both partners.
- **More self-prioritising** — willing to treat her career, time, pleasure, and ambitions as primary resources.
- **More comfortable receiving support** — able to delegate, be served, and allow her partner to carry domestic and emotional responsibility without guilt.
- **More sexually autonomous** — comfortable with her own desire, being desired, and making sexual choices for herself, including sex outside the marriage.
- **Less constrained by social conditioning** — less ruled by traditional femininity, shame, expected compromise, or fear of judgement.

### Male Partner
The man becomes:

- More monogamous and dedicated to her.
- Less competitive and less driven to demonstrate traditional masculine performance or dominance.
- More oriented toward service, caregiving, and anticipatory support.
- Comfortable (and eventually preferring) her taking the lead in decisions and sexual initiative.
- Sexually reframed around attentiveness to her pleasure and his usefulness rather than his own status or performance.
- Able to experience devotion and support as a legitimate positive identity rather than a loss or failure.

### Couple-Level Success Criterion
The relationship begins defaulting toward her direction and his support without continuous external prompting from programme staff.

## Multi-Domain Design Principle

Progression is tracked **separately by domain**.

A woman may advance rapidly in sexual autonomy while still showing residual guilt around receiving domestic service. A man may become comfortable with service tasks long before he internalises a non-competitive sexual identity.

Therefore:

- Each major goal area has its own progression ladder.
- Shared phase language (Recognition → Experiential Proof → Role Preference → Consolidation → Transfer) is used only as a common coordination skeleton.
- Pacing, methods, and intensity are domain-specific.
- Island scheduling must respect these different rates of change.

### Primary Domains (Female)
1. Authority & Decision Comfort
2. Self-Prioritisation (career, time, ambition)
3. Receiving Support & Reduced Guilt
4. Sexual Autonomy
5. Norm Resistance / Reduced Conditioning

### Primary Domains (Male)
1. Performance Relief & Reduced Competitiveness
2. Responsiveness to Direction
3. Service Orientation & Caregiving Competence
4. Comfort with Her Leadership
5. Sexual Reframing
6. Identity Shift (devotion as strength)

## Shared Phase Skeleton

These phases describe the general psychological movement within any single domain. They are not rigid global stages that every domain must occupy simultaneously.

| Phase | Female Movement | Male Movement |
|-------|------------------|---------------|
| **1. Recognition** | Begins questioning whether accommodation, domestic responsibility, and conventional femininity are actual obligations | Begins questioning whether leadership, sexual performance, independence, and conventional masculinity are obligations |
| **2. Experiential Proof** | Experiences directing, deciding, receiving service, being prioritised, and having others adapt to her | Experiences following, serving, caring, yielding decisions, and discovering that these can feel competent or relieving |
| **3. Role Preference** | Begins preferring authority rather than merely tolerating it | Begins preferring support/service rather than viewing it as loss or failure |
| **4. Consolidation** | Starts experiencing greater authority as part of who she is and something she is entitled to carry home | Starts experiencing caregiving and devotion as a legitimate positive identity |
| **5. Transfer** | Can articulate concrete changes she wants in career priority, household authority, intimacy, and autonomy | Can articulate concrete responsibilities he is willing to own and ways he will support her priorities |

## Key Performance Indicators

### Female KPIs
- **Decision comfort** — reduced hesitation before deciding for both partners
- **Receiving service** — reduced guilt when needs are met by others
- **Career entitlement** — increased willingness to treat her career and time as priority resources
- **Authority behaviour** — clearer instructions, boundaries, expectations; less permission-seeking
- **Sexual agency** — increased comfort acknowledging desire and treating sexual choice as hers
- **Norm resistance** — declining concern with whether behaviour is “proper” or traditionally feminine
- **Identity language** — shift from “I could maybe…” → “I want…” → “I expect…”

### Male KPIs
- **Performance relief** — reduced need to demonstrate masculinity, dominance, or constant competence
- **Responsiveness to direction** — increasing comfort accepting decisions without renegotiating control
- **Service satisfaction** — reports of pride or pleasure in being useful to her
- **Caregiving competence** — movement from completing assigned tasks toward anticipating needs and taking ownership
- **Reduced shame** — less embarrassment around tenderness, dependence, service, or submissive desires
- **Sexual reframing** — less focus on proving himself; more focus on attentiveness and her pleasure
- **Identity language** — begins describing devotion and support as strengths

### Couple-Level KPI
Does the interaction pattern begin defaulting to her direction + his support without staff having to prompt every instance?

## Methodological Notes

- Progression relies on repeated, structured experience rather than insight alone.
- Group modelling, observation of more advanced participants, and controlled social proof are used deliberately.
- Specialists (Real Men) are deployed primarily in the Sexual Autonomy and Authority domains under strict protocols.
- Husband presence and service functions are treated as environmental tools, not equal therapeutic targets in early phases.
- All interventions remain within the boundary of meaningful human agency. Coercion, threats of harm, or removal of choice are outside programme doctrine.

Detailed methods, specialist instructions, session structures, and measurement tools will be maintained in separate supporting documents.
