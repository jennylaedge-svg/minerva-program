**Parent document:** [[Psychological_Progression_Framework]]  
**Owner:** Psychological Monitoring Unit

## Purpose

This document details the domain-specific progression ladders for the male partner.  

Each domain moves through the shared five-phase skeleton (Recognition → Experiential Proof → Role Preference → Consolidation → Transfer) at its own pace and with its own methods. A participant will almost never occupy the same phase across all domains simultaneously.

The goal is measurable movement toward the male end-states defined in the parent framework: greater dedication and monogamy, reduced competitiveness, increased service orientation, comfort with her leadership, sexual reframing, and an identity that treats devotion as strength rather than loss.

---

## Domain 1: Performance Relief & Reduced Competitiveness

**End-state:** Reduced need to demonstrate traditional masculinity, dominance, sexual performance, or constant competence.

### Phase Ladder

**1. Recognition**  
- Identifies the internal pressure to lead, perform, and prove himself.  
- Begins to notice the cost of continuous self-monitoring and competition.  
- Method focus: performance-pressure inventory, guided reflection on relief moments.

**2. Experiential Proof**  
- Experiences structured situations in which he is not required to lead, decide, or sexually perform.  
- Discovers that the absence of performance demand can feel calming rather than humiliating.  
- Method focus: deliberate non-leadership exercises, specialist-led sexual sessions in which his role is peripheral or service-oriented, staff modelling of non-competitive male behaviour.

**3. Role Preference**  
- Begins to prefer the lower cognitive and emotional load of not competing.  
- Shows reduced urge to reassert control or status when it is not requested.  
- Method focus: repeated non-performance conditions, positive reinforcement of relaxed presence.

**4. Consolidation**  
- Experiences reduced performance drive as a stable internal state rather than a temporary programme effect.  
- Language shifts away from self-justification toward simple acceptance.  
- Method focus: identity framing, reduced external scaffolding.

**5. Transfer**  
- Articulates specific areas in which he will no longer compete for leadership or sexual status at home.  
- Method focus: concrete relief protocols and relapse-prevention planning.

## Domain 2: Responsiveness to Direction

**End-state:** Increasing comfort accepting her decisions and instructions without needing to renegotiate control.

### Phase Ladder

**1. Recognition**  
- Notices automatic impulses to question, improve, or take over her decisions.  
- Begins to see these impulses as conditioning rather than necessary competence.  
- Method focus: decision-response diaries, language audits.

**2. Experiential Proof**  
- Practices immediate, non-negotiating compliance with clear directions in low- and medium-stakes situations.  
- Experiences that following competent direction can feel efficient and relieving.  
- Method focus: structured compliance drills, specialist and staff direction exercises, husband-service protocols linked to her authority practice.

**3. Role Preference**  
- Begins to prefer the clarity of receiving direction over the effort of constant joint decision-making.  
- Shows reduced internal resistance when instructions are given.  
- Method focus: increasing frequency and scope of directed tasks, reinforcement of clean responsiveness.

**4. Consolidation**  
- Responds to her direction as a normal and appropriate pattern.  
- Experiences pride or calm in clean execution rather than in having input.  
- Method focus: identity language (“I follow well”), reduced prompting.

**5. Transfer**  
- Defines the domains in which her direction will be default at home and how he will signal acceptance.  
- Method focus: written responsiveness agreements.

## Domain 3: Service Orientation & Caregiving Competence

**End-state:** Moves from completing assigned tasks toward anticipating needs and taking ownership of support; reports pride or pleasure in being useful to her.

### Phase Ladder

**1. Recognition**  
- Identifies the difference between performing isolated chores and carrying real responsibility for her comfort and the household.  
- Begins to see service as a skill rather than a demotion.  
- Method focus: responsibility-mapping, observation of advanced male participants.

**2. Experiential Proof**  
- Practices both reactive service (responding to requests) and proactive service (anticipating and acting).  
- Receives clear feedback on quality and attitude.  
- Method focus: graduated service protocols, real-time coaching, structured caregiving tasks during her priority time or sexual sessions.

**3. Role Preference**  
- Begins to take satisfaction from successful anticipation and smooth support.  
- Shows initiative without being asked.  
- Method focus: extended service periods, positive reinforcement of anticipatory behaviour, mild status among male participants for high-quality service.

**4. Consolidation**  
- Experiences caregiving competence as a core part of his contribution and identity.  
- Reduced need for external validation of each act.  
- Method focus: identity integration, reduced coaching.

**5. Transfer**  
- Specifies the ongoing service and household responsibilities he will own at home and the standards he will maintain.  
- Method focus: detailed service and responsibility protocols for home use.

## Domain 4: Comfort with Her Leadership

**End-state:** Comfortable (and eventually preferring) her taking the lead in decisions, direction, and overall relationship framing.

### Phase Ladder

**1. Recognition**  
- Questions the assumption that male leadership is the default or the measure of his worth.  
- Begins to separate his value from the leadership role.  
- Method focus: leadership-assumption inventory, exposure to alternative male models within the programme.

**2. Experiential Proof**  
- Experiences extended periods in which she holds clear leadership and he functions effectively within that frame.  
- Discovers that the relationship can feel stable and even more functional under her direction.  
- Method focus: authority exercises paired with his responsiveness practice, observation of advanced couples.

**3. Role Preference**  
- Begins to prefer the felt sense of her leadership over the previous pattern.  
- Shows reduced nostalgia for equal or male-led framing.  
- Method focus: repeated leadership configurations, reinforcement of positive affect linked to her lead.

**4. Consolidation**  
- Experiences her leadership as the natural and desirable structure.  
- Internal resistance becomes minimal.  
- Method focus: identity language, future-pacing.

**5. Transfer**  
- Articulates how her leadership will be expressed and supported in daily life at home.  
- Method focus: concrete leadership-support agreements.

## Domain 5: Sexual Reframing

**End-state:** Less focus on proving himself sexually; more focus on attentiveness, her pleasure, and his usefulness. Comfortable with her sexual autonomy, including external partners.

### Phase Ladder

**1. Recognition**  
- Identifies performance-based sexual identity and the link between sexual access and masculine status.  
- Begins to separate his worth from being her sole or primary sexual partner.  
- Method focus: sexual-identity inventory, non-shaming discussion of comparative dynamics.

**2. Experiential Proof**  
- Experiences structured sexual situations in which his role is supportive, observational, or service-oriented while she receives pleasure from a specialist.  
- Practices attentiveness and post-encounter service without being the central sexual actor.  
- Method focus: staged specialist sessions with defined male roles, immediate reframing of arousal and affect, observation of advanced male participants.

**3. Role Preference**  
- Begins to find meaning or arousal in her pleasure and his supportive function.  
- Shows reduced comparative distress and increased focus on her experience.  
- Method focus: repeated supportive sexual roles, reinforcement of service-oriented sexual behaviour, hierarchical framing during encounters.

**4. Consolidation**  
- Experiences her sexual autonomy as compatible with (or enhancing of) his role.  
- Performance pressure is significantly reduced.  
- Method focus: identity integration, reduced novelty framing.

**5. Transfer**  
- Articulates the sexual role and rules he will maintain at home, including his stance toward her external sexual choices.  
- Method focus: explicit sexual protocol design.

**Special notes**  
This domain is closely linked to the female Sexual Autonomy track. Pacing must be coordinated. Early exposure is carefully graded; forced intensity is counterproductive.

## Domain 6: Identity Shift (Devotion as Strength)

**End-state:** Begins seeing devotion, support, and caregiving as legitimate strengths rather than concessions or failures.

### Phase Ladder

**1. Recognition**  
- Notices the cultural script that equates male worth with dominance, independence, and sexual primacy.  
- Begins to question whether that script fits him.  
- Method focus: cultural-script inventory, exposure to alternative male identities within the programme.

**2. Experiential Proof**  
- Collects repeated experiences in which devoted, supportive behaviour produces positive relational outcomes and internal calm.  
- Method focus: success tracking of service and responsiveness, peer modelling by advanced men.

**3. Role Preference**  
- Begins to prefer the identity of the devoted, competent supporter.  
- Feels pride in that role.  
- Method focus: identity language practice, status reinforcement among male participants for high-quality devotion.

**4. Consolidation**  
- Experiences the supportive identity as authentic and valuable.  
- Residual shame is minimal.  
- Method focus: narrative integration, reduced external validation.

**5. Transfer**  
- Can describe his intended identity and contribution at home in positive, non-apologetic terms.  
- Method focus: personal identity statement and home implementation plan.

## Cross-Domain Coordination Rules

1. Performance Relief and Sexual Reframing often move together; progress in one supports the other.  
2. Responsiveness to Direction is a prerequisite for high-quality Service Orientation.  
3. Comfort with Her Leadership is accelerated by successful experiences in Authority practice on the female side.  
4. Identity Shift is the slowest domain for most men and should not be forced early.  
5. No domain advances by insight alone. Every phase after Recognition requires structured, repeated experience.

## Measurement

Each domain is scored on the relevant male KPIs from the parent framework at intake, mid-programme, and pre-departure.  
Narrative notes focus on language shifts, initiation of service, residual competitiveness or shame, and affective responses to her leadership and sexual autonomy.

Detailed session structures, specialist protocols, and measurement instruments are maintained in the Methods Catalogue.
