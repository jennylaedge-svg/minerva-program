# Participant-Facing Staffing Model

## Purpose

This document defines the basic staffing structure used around couples during Minerva retreats.

The island should feel like a professionally run luxury programme, not like each participant has a single obvious handler.

---

## Lead Relationship Development Coach

Each couple has one primary **Lead Relationship Development Coach**, usually female in Channel 1.

She:

- knows the full couple profile;
- runs important joint sessions;
- also meets both partners individually;
- acts as the couple's main trusted Minerva contact;
- coordinates input from the wider team.

Major progression decisions are not hers alone.

---

## Supporting Development Coaches

The Lead Coach is supported by other coaches.

These may include:

- male coaches for husband-focused work around masculinity, competition, jealousy, caregiving, or role identity;
- female coaches for wife-focused work around authority, ambition, guilt, sexuality, or group sessions;
- specialist coaches for career, intimacy, domestic-role development, or other programme areas.

Participants should encounter multiple Minerva staff so the programme feels institutional rather than centred on one person.

---

## Clinical and Assessment Team

Psychologists and behavioural staff work largely behind the scenes.

They:

- review assessments;
- analyse participant responses;
- advise coaches;
- monitor readiness and risk;
- contribute to progression decisions.

They do not need constant direct contact with participants.

---

## Case Team and Daily Review

Each couple has a small internal case team.

Observations from:

- coaching sessions;
- group activities;
- hospitality staff;
- embedded specialists;
- assessments;
- informal participant behaviour

can be brought into daily case review.

The team decides whether to:

- reinforce a current stage;
- slow progression;
- introduce a new topic;
- create an opportunity;
- change which staff interact with the couple.

Major interventions should be coordinated rather than improvised by individual employees.

---

## General Island Staff

All participant-facing island staff are deliberately selected.

This includes:

- reception;
- restaurant staff;
- bartenders;
- housekeeping;
- spa staff;
- boat crews;
- activity staff;
- maintenance and support staff.

They sign confidentiality agreements and receive basic Minerva orientation.

They understand:

- the island is a relationship-development environment;
- participant privacy is essential;
- ordinary interactions can affect the programme;
- unusual behaviour or concerns should be reported;
- they should not invent their own interventions.

Most general staff do not receive full couple profiles.

---

## Programme-Aware Hospitality Staff

Some hospitality staff have greater programme awareness.

They may receive simple case-specific guidance, for example:

- encourage the wife to state her own preference;
- avoid automatically addressing the husband first;
- create opportunities for one partner to make decisions;
- observe reactions to particular situations.

Their visible role remains ordinary resort work.

---

## Embedded Programme Specialists

Some staff have normal-looking island roles while also serving deliberate programme functions.

Examples include:

- bartenders;
- gym trainers;
- massage staff;
- watersports instructors;
- activity guides;
- boat staff.

Selected attractive, confident men may function internally as **Social Activation Specialists** or **External Experience Specialists**, depending on their assignment.

Their programme role may include:

- awakening attraction in wives;
- providing contrast with supportive husbands;
- creating mild status or competitive tension;
- giving staff useful behavioural observations;
- later participating in explicitly authorised programme experiences.

They do not independently decide how far to push a couple.

---

## Male Exemplars and Alumni

Minerva may also use:

- male alumni who visibly thrive in supportive roles;
- returning couples further along in the programme;
- female alumni who model authority and confidence.

Their purpose is to make Minerva's desired relationship patterns look lived, functional, affectionate, and credible.

---

## Operational Principle

The retreat should feel consistent without appearing centrally orchestrated.

Participants encounter the same underlying worldview through:

- coaches;
- other couples;
- ordinary staff behaviour;
- structured activities;
- social interactions;
- the physical environment.

The institution shapes the experience.

No single employee should appear to be controlling the entire relationship.
