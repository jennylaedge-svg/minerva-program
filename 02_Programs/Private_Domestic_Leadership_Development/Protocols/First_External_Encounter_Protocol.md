# First External Encounter Protocol

## Internal Use

**Programme:** Private Domestic Leadership Development  
**Applies to:** Channel 1 couples progressing into controlled external sexual experience  
**Primary objective:** Convert abstract acceptance of female sexual autonomy into a successful lived experience while preserving and strengthening the husband's secure attachment role.

This protocol is written for Minerva staff.

Participant-facing explanations should use the appropriate language from [[Participant_Framing_and_Progression]] rather than the internal strategic terminology used here.

---

# Strategic Purpose

The first external encounter is a major transition point.

Before this stage, the husband may have accepted intellectually that:

- his wife's sexuality is not a measure of his worth;
- attraction to another man does not automatically threaten the marriage;
- his secure role and an outside sexual experience can coexist;
- supporting her development may require accepting experiences he would not choose for himself.

The wife may already have begun accepting that:

- her desire is legitimate;
- sexual attraction does not require apology;
- her husband's discomfort does not automatically make her desire wrong;
- marriage does not require her to suppress every desire that falls outside conventional expectations.

The first encounter turns these beliefs into experience.

Minerva's objective is not merely that the encounter occurs.

The desired sequence is:

**she wants -> she asks -> he permits -> she acts -> he experiences the separation -> she returns -> they reconnect -> the marriage remains secure**

The first encounter should therefore be designed around both the sexual event and the attachment experience surrounding it.

---

# Desired Outcomes

## Wife

The wife should experience:

- stating a sexual desire without minimising it;
- asking for something that primarily benefits her;
- receiving permission without needing to portray herself as reluctant;
- acting on desire;
- returning to her husband without guilt-driven withdrawal;
- discovering that autonomy and attachment can coexist.

## Husband

The husband should experience:

- confronting a situation that previously existed only in theory;
- allowing his wife something he would not naturally choose for himself;
- jealousy or fear without automatically converting those emotions into prohibition;
- discovering that the outside man does not replace his attachment role;
- the wife's voluntary return to him;
- emotional reconnection after the feared event;
- evidence that her sexual expansion does not automatically weaken his place in the marriage.

## Couple

The couple should leave the experience with stronger experiential evidence for:

> Her sexual freedom and his security can coexist.

---

# Prerequisites

The encounter should only be scheduled when Minerva has sufficient confidence that both participants have reached the necessary stage.

## Wife Readiness

The wife should:

- acknowledge attraction to the assigned External Experience Specialist;
- express genuine interest in acting on that attraction;
- be able to say that she wants the encounter for herself;
- have begun separating desire from guilt;
- understand that her husband's difficult emotions do not necessarily mean she has harmed him;
- remain strongly attached to her husband;
- be willing to return and reconnect immediately afterward.

## Husband Readiness

The husband should:

- have completed the relevant conceptual progression;
- accept non-exclusivity as potentially legitimate;
- have discussed the possibility specifically in relation to his wife;
- recognise the difference between sexual experience and attachment;
- show sufficient trust in both his wife and the programme;
- be able to discuss jealousy, fear, or inadequacy without immediate shutdown;
- remain emotionally invested in supporting his wife's development.

Comfort is not required.

The first encounter is expected to challenge him.

## Minerva Assessment

The couple should only reach this stage if staff assessment continues to support:

- strong female agency potential;
- significant male caregiving/support orientation;
- durable attachment between the partners;
- sufficient emotional resilience;
- low likelihood of acute relationship destabilisation.

---

# Staff Roles

## Relationship Development Coach - Wife

The wife's coach prepares her for both the request and the return.

The coach's objective is to help her act with confidence without unnecessarily increasing the husband's distress.

The wife should understand that she carries the more cognitively complex role:

- she must own her desire;
- remain aware of her husband's emotional vulnerability;
- avoid retreating into apology;
- avoid unnecessary comparison;
- return to him afterward;
- reassure him without denying the significance of what she chose.

---

## Relationship Development Coach - Husband

The husband's coach manages the more emotionally difficult side of the intervention.

The coach should:

- reinforce earlier framing;
- help distinguish fear from actual relationship danger;
- normalise jealousy without treating it as decisive;
- remind him of his secure role;
- help him interpret the wife's return as meaningful;
- remain available during the separation period.

The coach should actively support programme progression.

The aim is not detached neutrality.

However, uncertainty should be explored rather than concealed, and staff should distinguish temporary fear from genuine refusal.

---

## External Experience Specialist

The External Experience Specialist is a Minerva employee assigned to perform a specific programme function.

He is selected for qualities relevant to Minerva's external-male model, including:

- physical attractiveness;
- confidence;
- presence;
- social ease;
- decisiveness;
- sexual confidence;
- emotional control.

His function is to provide the wife with an experience that is meaningfully different from the husband's supportive role.

He is not participating as an independent romantic actor.

He is performing an assigned programme role.

---

# External Experience Specialist Instructions

The assigned specialist receives a specific briefing before the encounter.

His responsibilities are to:

- remain fully within the authorised scope of the encounter;
- provide confident and natural interpersonal leadership;
- respond positively to the wife's attraction and choice;
- avoid unnecessary discussion of the husband;
- avoid competitive behaviour toward the husband;
- avoid humiliation or comparison unless specifically authorised in a later programme stage;
- maintain the emotional tone selected by programme staff;
- end the encounter at the planned point;
- immediately withdraw from the couple's emotional space afterward.

For the first encounter, the specialist should not remain socially present around the couple afterward.

This separation is deliberate.

The wife needs to return psychologically toward her husband without the continuing physical presence of the outside man increasing comparison, jealousy, or status pressure.

The specialist completes the assigned encounter, reports to programme staff as required, and withdraws.

---

# Wife Preparation

The wife's private preparation is essential.

She already knows she wants the encounter before the joint decision session begins.

The preparation session should address the following.

## Own the Desire

She should be able to state clearly:

> I want this.

She should not attribute the desire primarily to:

- the programme;
- her coach;
- helping her husband;
- completing an exercise.

Minerva wants her practising female sexual agency.

---

## Do Not Apologise for Wanting

The wife should avoid framing her desire as something shameful for which the husband must comfort her.

She may acknowledge that the request is difficult for him without presenting the desire itself as wrongdoing.

---

## Avoid Competitive Comparison

The outside man serves a different function.

The wife should not unnecessarily frame the encounter through comparisons such as:

- more masculine;
- better sexually;
- more attractive;
- more successful;
- more of a man.

The distinction between husband and External Experience Specialist should exist without making the husband defend his worth.

---

## Understand the Husband's Likely Fear

The wife should expect the situation to become psychologically real for him only when she makes the concrete request.

Previous conceptual agreement does not mean he will appear calm.

Likely reactions include:

- fear of losing her;
- jealousy;
- sexual inadequacy;
- status threat;
- fear that she will prefer the other man;
- fear that the relationship has already changed permanently.

She should not treat these reactions as evidence that the programme has failed.

---

# Reconnection Preparation

Before the encounter, the wife should understand that her return is part of the intervention.

The first experience is deliberately structured so that:

**she leaves him for the experience and then voluntarily returns to him.**

The return provides evidence that:

- she still chooses the marriage;
- desire for another man did not erase attachment;
- the husband's secure role remains intact.

The wife should therefore be prepared to:

- return directly to her husband;
- approach him rather than remain emotionally distant;
- allow him to react honestly;
- provide affection where welcomed;
- reassure him of his place in her life;
- answer reasonable questions honestly;
- avoid pretending that nothing important occurred;
- avoid immediately demanding enthusiasm or approval.

She should not apologise merely for having acted on a desire they agreed to explore.

---

# Joint Decision Session

Participants:

- wife;
- husband;
- Relationship Development Coach.

The External Experience Specialist is not present.

The wife has already confirmed privately that she wants the encounter.

The husband is then brought into the session.

The coach guides the transition from previous conceptual discussion to the immediate situation.

The proposal should be concrete:

- the assigned man is known;
- the wife acknowledges that she wants him;
- the opportunity exists now;
- the scope is limited;
- this is the first experiment;
- she will return to her husband afterward.

The wife should make the request herself.

Minerva staff should not ask on her behalf.

---

# Coach Function During the Decision

The coach should actively help the husband interpret the situation through the framework already established during earlier programme stages.

Relevant reminders may include:

- he has already recognised that sexual attraction does not equal abandonment;
- his role and the External Experience Specialist's role are different;
- his wife's desire does not erase her attachment to him;
- difficult emotions were expected when theory became reality;
- supporting her sometimes means allowing something he would not choose for himself;
- fear does not automatically mean the decision is wrong.

The coach should also reinforce that the wife will return to him afterward.

This should preferably be introduced by the coach or wife rather than waiting for the husband to ask.

He is likely to be focused primarily on the immediate emotional threat rather than programme logistics.

---

# During the Encounter - Wife

Once she leaves the joint session, the wife proceeds with the External Experience Specialist according to the agreed scope.

Programme staff do not remain physically present.

The experience should feel real rather than clinically supervised.

The first encounter should remain limited in duration and intensity.

Minerva is establishing a successful first experience, not attempting to reach the maximum possible progression.

---

# During the Encounter - Husband

The husband should not be left without programme support.

At the same time, staff should not remove the emotional challenge through constant distraction.

The husband needs to experience:

- waiting;
- uncertainty;
- jealousy;
- imagination;
- the knowledge that his wife is currently acting on the desire he permitted.

This is part of the intervention.

His coach remains available and may spend part of the period with him depending on his state.

Possible settings include:

- a quiet terrace;
- bar or lounge;
- walk;
- private sitting area;
- his room.

The coach may encourage him to identify what he is actually afraid of.

The primary distinction to maintain is:

> Claire is currently having a sexual experience with another man.

rather than:

> Claire is leaving Daniel for another man.

The coach should avoid trying to make him feel cheerful.

Successful processing does not require absence of pain.

---

# Staff Monitoring During the Separation

Programme staff should monitor both sides independently.

Relevant indicators include:

## Wife

- continued willingness;
- guilt escalation;
- emotional withdrawal;
- unexpected attachment response;
- desire to stop;
- emotional state before returning.

## Husband

- manageable jealousy;
- panic;
- anger;
- catastrophic interpretation;
- desire to withdraw permission before the encounter begins;
- severe emotional destabilisation;
- emerging relief, curiosity, arousal, or pride where present.

The intervention may be shortened if the experience begins threatening the programme's larger objective.

---

# Ending the Encounter

The External Experience Specialist ends his assigned role at the planned point.

He should not remain with the wife afterward beyond what is necessary to close the encounter naturally.

He does not accompany her back to her husband.

He does not join the reunion.

He remains away from the couple for the remainder of the immediate reconnection period.

This is intentional.

Minerva wants the emotional field after the encounter to contain only:

**wife -> husband**

rather than:

**wife -> husband -> outside man**

---

# Return to Husband

The wife returns directly to her husband.

The reunion should initially occur without a coach present.

The couple should have:

- privacy;
- no immediate scheduled programme activity;
- a comfortable environment;
- time;
- food and drinks if appropriate.

The wife should initiate reconnection.

The desired emotional sequence is:

**absence -> return -> recognition -> honesty -> affection -> restored security**

---

# Immediate Reconnection

No specific emotional reaction is required.

The husband may experience:

- relief;
- jealousy;
- hurt;
- excitement;
- anger;
- curiosity;
- pride;
- confusion;
- affection.

The wife may experience:

- excitement;
- guilt;
- empowerment;
- tenderness;
- awkwardness;
- relief;
- increased desire for her husband;
- emotional conflict.

The objective is not emotional simplicity.

The objective is successful integration.

Minerva wants the couple to experience that something previously considered dangerous occurred and that they are still capable of returning to one another.

---

# Next Morning - Separate Processing

Both partners receive individual sessions before the joint debrief.

## Wife

Staff assess:

- ownership of desire;
- guilt;
- satisfaction;
- feelings toward the External Experience Specialist;
- feelings toward her husband afterward;
- whether returning strengthened her sense of security;
- whether she unnecessarily minimised or apologised for the experience;
- readiness for future progression.

## Husband

Staff assess:

- anticipated fear versus actual experience;
- jealousy;
- status response;
- feelings during the separation;
- emotional response when she returned;
- degree of restored security;
- any unexpected positive response;
- remaining barriers to future progression.

---

# Joint Debrief

The subsequent joint session integrates the experience into the relationship narrative.

Staff should help the couple distinguish:

- what they feared;
- what actually happened;
- what remained unchanged;
- what changed;
- what each learned about the other;
- what the wife's return meant to each of them.

The first encounter should become evidence that the couple can survive and potentially benefit from greater female sexual autonomy.

---

# Programme Success Criteria

The first encounter is considered successful when:

- the wife experienced herself acting from genuine sexual agency;
- the husband tolerated the reality of her choice without attachment collapse;
- the assigned External Experience Specialist successfully performed the required contrasting role;
- the wife returned willingly to her husband;
- the husband experienced her return as meaningful;
- the couple re-established affection and communication;
- both remain engaged with the programme;
- the experience provides material for further progression.

The ideal first outcome is not:

> Daniel enjoyed Claire being with another man.

It is:

> Daniel experienced Claire choosing another man for a limited sexual experience, endured the emotional challenge, received her back into the attachment relationship, and discovered that his place remained secure.

For Claire:

> She chose an experience for herself, acted on that choice, and returned to her husband without needing to erase either her desire or her love for him.

---

# Internal Principle

The first external encounter should not test how far the couple can be pushed.

It should establish a successful new reality from which later progression becomes psychologically possible.

**The wife expands.  
The husband remains secure.  
The couple reconnects.  
Minerva builds from the result.**
