**INTERNAL MEMO**  
**From:** Head of Human Resources  
**To:** HR Team – Island & Programme Operations  
**Date:** 3 oktober 2026
**Subject:** Monitoring staff tone and attitudes toward male participants  
**Classification:** Internal – HR only

---

We are seeing a noticeable uptick in staff members who appear to derive personal satisfaction from the degradation or humiliation of male participants (particularly the husbands).

This is not surprising. The nature of the work selects for dominant personalities, and a degree of controlled severity is operationally useful in later stages of husband training. A certain amount of private derogatory talk and quiet enjoyment of power differentials is therefore expected and, within limits, tolerated.

What we are tracking is the risk that this becomes a broader staff culture.

**Examples of the kind of comments and behaviour already noted:**
- Private remarks such as “another useless one”, “look at him fold”, or “she should have left him years ago”
- Visible pleasure taken in a husband’s discomfort during early sessions, beyond what the protocol requires
- Side conversations that frame all soft or supportive men as inherently contemptible rather than as programme material
- Occasional unnecessary harshness in tone or body language during husband intake or the first 48 hours

Isolated instances are fine. A developing culture in which weak or supportive men are secretly hated is poison to the programme. It will eventually leak into participant-facing moments, especially when new husbands arrive and need to be put at ease rather than put on edge.

**Action for the HR team**
1. Quietly track frequency and intensity of this tone across teams.
2. Identify individuals who show the strongest or most consistent pattern.
3. Report internally (to me) if the pattern appears to be spreading beyond a few personalities into a general atmosphere.
4. No formal intervention is required at this stage unless it begins to affect participant experience or core programme delivery.

The mission stays central. Personal gratification is secondary. We need people strong enough to hold the line with male participants when the protocol calls for it — not people who need to degrade them in order to feel effective.

Monitor and keep me updated.

Jane Greport
Head of Human Resources
