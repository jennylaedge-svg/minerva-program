# Minerva Funding Model

Minerva operates through a mixed funding model designed to support both its public mission and its long-term institutional independence.

## Institutional Funding

A substantial part of Minerva's work is funded through grants and programme funding from external organisations.

Potential sources include:

- National governments and government programmes
- International and multilateral institutions
- Non-governmental organisations
- Charitable foundations
- Development and gender-equality funds
- Other institutional partners

These organisations fund Minerva because its programmes are presented as advancing gender equality, women's agency, and women's participation in professional and public leadership.

Different grants may be restricted to particular programmes, populations, countries, research activities, or outcomes.

## Founder and Private Funding

Minerva was originally established with substantial financial backing from a small number of extremely wealthy women.

These women are connected to Minerva's origins and may continue to provide funding or financial support.

Their identities, motivations, continuing influence, and relationship with the present organisation remain to be developed.

This source of funding potentially gives Minerva a degree of independence from governments and institutional donors.

## Paid Services

Minerva also receives income directly from some of the people who use its services.

For example, a woman seeking Minerva's assistance with changing the balance of responsibility, decision-making, or power within her household may pay for Minerva's involvement.

The exact nature of these services, how they are priced, and who formally becomes the client remain to be developed.

## Financial Controls

The combination of grants and paid services creates accounting and governance issues.

Minerva may receive institutional funding for activities similar to services for which individual clients are also charged. The organisation therefore needs mechanisms for:

- Separating restricted grant funding from commercial or service income
- Allocating programme costs correctly
- Preventing or concealing double funding, depending on how Minerva actually operates
- Demonstrating appropriate use of grant money to funders and auditors
- Managing payments from private clients
- Protecting the organisation from financial scrutiny that could expose its less public activities

Minerva therefore requires a sophisticated finance, legal, and compliance function.

## Public Face and Internal Reality

Publicly, Minerva presents its funding as supporting legitimate programmes intended to advance gender equality and women's agency.

Internally, Minerva may be considerably more ruthless about achieving its objectives than its public presentation suggests.

How far this extends into its financial practices is currently undecided.

## Open Questions

- Who were Minerva's original wealthy founders?
- Are they still involved?
- How financially dependent is Minerva on them?
- What proportion of Minerva's income comes from grants versus clients?
- Who legally pays for individual interventions?
- What exactly is a paying client purchasing?
- How transparent is Minerva with institutional funders about paid services?
- Are Minerva's accounts genuinely compliant, aggressively structured, or deliberately deceptive?
- Who inside Minerva controls the money?
