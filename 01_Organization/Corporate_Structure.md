# Minerva Corporate Structure

Minerva operates through a network of nonprofit, commercial, and asset-holding entities rather than through a single organisation.

The structure allows grant-funded public programmes, privately paid services, research, media activity, and retreat operations to remain financially and operationally separated while still contributing to Minerva's broader objectives.

## Founder Network

Minerva was originally created and financed by a small group of extremely wealthy women.

The founders remain an important source of financial security, strategic continuity, and influence.

They are closely associated with the commercial side of the wider Minerva network and could provide substantial funding if outside grants or programme income were lost.

Their exact formal role in the governance of the Minerva Foundation remains to be established.

They may exercise influence partly through trusted associates, long-standing relationships, appointments, and informal networks rather than through direct ownership or explicit control.

## Minerva Foundation

The Minerva Foundation is the principal public-facing nonprofit organisation.

It receives:

- Government funding
- NGO and institutional grants
- Foundation funding
- Donations
- Other forms of programme and research funding

Its activities include:

- Women's leadership programmes
- Gender-equality programmes
- Household-agency initiatives
- Research
- Advocacy
- Institutional partnerships
- Public-facing educational work

The Foundation is not owned by the founders in the commercial sense.

Its governance structure is formally independent, although the founders and their network have historically exercised substantial influence over its development, appointments, and strategic direction.

The precise composition and mechanisms of control within the Foundation's board remain to be developed.

## Minerva Holdings

Minerva Holdings represents the commercial and asset-owning side of the wider Minerva network.

It is separate from the nonprofit Foundation.

The founder network has substantially more direct financial control over this side of the organisation.

Its subsidiaries can own property, provide paid services, operate facilities, and conduct activities that would be inappropriate or unnecessarily complicated to place directly inside the nonprofit Foundation.

## Property Company

A dedicated property company owns major physical assets used by Minerva.

These may include:

- The private retreat island
- Buildings and accommodation
- Boats
- Transport infrastructure
- Utilities
- Specialist facilities

Separating ownership from daily operations protects valuable assets from operational liabilities and allows the facilities to be used by multiple Minerva-related organisations.

## Retreat Operations

A separate operating company runs the island and related retreat facilities.

It employs or contracts ordinary operational staff such as:

- Hospitality staff
- Catering staff
- Cleaning and maintenance teams
- Boat crews
- Spa and recreation staff
- Technical personnel
- Guest services

The operating company provides accommodation, transport, meals, facilities, and other services to organisations running programmes on the island.

Many ordinary employees may know little about Minerva's deeper organisational structure or objectives.

Programme specialists may be employed or contracted separately from ordinary hospitality staff.

## Programme Services

Commercial programme companies provide paid services to private clients.

These can include relationship programmes, coaching, specialised interventions, premium retreats, and other services that are not primarily funded through grants.

Commercial programmes may use the same island infrastructure as nonprofit programmes while paying for that use through separate contracts and accounts.

## Media, Consumer Brands, and Recruitment

Minerva may operate or finance multiple consumer-facing brands, publications, assessments, advice platforms, and research initiatives.

These brands do not necessarily advertise their connection to Minerva prominently.

A participant might therefore encounter a relationship article, questionnaire, research project, advice platform, or specialised programme without immediately recognising that several of them ultimately belong to the same wider organisational network.

The connections are not necessarily secret, but they may require deliberate investigation to uncover.

## Research

Research may be conducted through Minerva itself, specialised research entities, academic partnerships, or funded external projects.

Research supports:

- Public legitimacy
- Programme development
- Participant selection
- Advertising and messaging
- Institutional funding
- Evaluation of outcomes

The exact legal position of Minerva's research activities within the wider structure remains open.

## Movement of Money

Different parts of the Minerva network use separate funding routes.

A simplified grant-funded route may be:

Government, NGO, or foundation funding  
→ Minerva Foundation  
→ programme budget  
→ contracted retreat or programme services  
→ payment for accommodation, transport, facilities, staff, and delivery costs

A commercial route may be:

Private client  
→ commercial programme company  
→ retreat operator and other service providers

The same physical infrastructure may therefore support both grant-funded and privately paid programmes while the financial flows remain formally separate.

Transactions between related Minerva entities create potential conflicts of interest and require careful accounting, pricing, contracting, and governance.

Minerva employs highly capable lawyers, accountants, and financial specialists to ensure these arrangements can withstand external scrutiny.

## Relationship Between the Public and Commercial Sides

The Minerva Foundation and Minerva Holdings are legally and financially distinct, but they ultimately exist within the same broader ecosystem.

The Foundation provides legitimacy, institutional relationships, research, public programmes, and access to grant funding.

The commercial side provides assets, infrastructure, paid services, operational flexibility, and an independent financial base.

The founder network forms an important connection between the two.

The exact legal ownership structure, board composition, jurisdiction of incorporation, and degree of formal separation between specific entities remain to be developed.
